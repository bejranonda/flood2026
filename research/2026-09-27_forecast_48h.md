# Forecasting 48 h ahead: what our data supports, HII's official forecast, and which models to build next

> **Date:** 2026-09-27 (evening UTC) · **Validity:** 🟢 measured on our own archive; every number below comes from a
> script in [validation/](validation/) that anyone can re-run. **Single 45-day test window** (late Aug – late Sep 2026,
> flood season): treat gains as strong hints, not final proof, until repeated on more windows (§6).
> **Asked by the owner:** "Change 12/24 h to 24/48 h? Say 'stable for 48 h' when no fall is forecast? Is HII's forecast
> sensible, or is our own model more comprehensive? Should forecast rain be in the model? Should we use space-time
> autoregression, SSN, k-NN, GTWR or ST-GNN?"
> **Decisions taken from this:** [D-050](../docs/plan/DECISIONS.md) · methods summary in [APPROACH §20](../docs/APPROACH_AND_METHODS.md).

## 1. How far ahead the current model is useful (production backtest, 102 gauges, 2026-09-27)

| Horizon | Gauges whose model beats "no change" by ≥ 30 % | Median 50 % band | Median 90 % band |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 h | 37 | 14 cm | 46 cm |
| 24 h | 18 | 15 cm | 47 cm |
| 48 h | **7** | 27 cm | **80 cm** |
| 72 h | 7 | 35 cm | 109 cm |

The 7 gauges with 48 h skill are tidal river/estuary gauges (THA009, BKC002, BKC003, CPY015, BKK006, C.12, T.14). Bangkok's
canal gauges have no model that beats "no change" at any horizon. Reason: the production model knows only the gauge's
own past (tide harmonics + recent trend) — no rain, no upstream flow, no dam release.

**Consequence (D-050):** keep 12 h + 24 h; add a 48 h line only where the 48 h backtest passes. When no fall is
forecast, say "ยังไม่เห็นแนวโน้มลดลงใน 24 ชม. ข้างหน้า" — never "stable for 48 h": for most gauges the 48 h range is wider
than the distance to the bank, so "stable" would be a promise the data cannot keep.

## 2. HII's official forecast — found, archived, first checks

- **Where:** `https://fews2.hii.or.th/model-output/data_portal/hii_waterlevel/forecast/{CODE}.txt` (levels, m MSL) and
  `…/rid_discharge/forecast/{CODE}.txt` (m³/s). Found in the ONWR "National Thai Water" app bundle, which reads them
  through a thaiwater.net proxy; we read the files directly (keyless, honest UA).
- **Coverage near us:** levels at CPY011 Ayutthaya, CPY014 Nonthaburi (Nuanchawee), PAS008 Tha Ruea; discharge at C.13,
  C.2, C.3, C.7A, C.35 (+ ~60 other stations nationally). Nothing for Bangkok canals.
- **Format:** hourly, Thai time, `station,date,time,value`; each file spans ~6 days before the issue time to 7 days after.
  Issue time = the file's `Last-Modified` (10:05 UTC = 17:05 ICT on 2026-09-27). **Overwritten on each issue.**
- **Datum/time check:** before the issue time the series matches our observations to MAE 1.1 cm (CPY011) and 2.8 cm
  (CPY014) — i.e. that part is observed/assimilated, **not a forecast**, and proves nothing about skill. In the first
  6 h after issue HII was within 1–4 cm on the tidal CPY014 (good tide timing).
- **Assumption found:** the C.13 dam discharge forecast is **flat at 1,950 m³/s for 7 days** — HII assumes today's release
  continues. Downstream levels are therefore *conditional on the dam release staying as it is*; any display must say so.
- **Is it more comprehensive than ours?** Very likely yes on the main river: it behaves like a river model fed by upstream
  flows and dam release (⚠️ inferred from behaviour, not from HII documentation). Ours is per-gauge statistics. Ours wins
  on coverage (102 gauges) and on being openly backtested.
- **How to prove it (built, D-050):** collector `hii_fews_forecast` archives every new issue (8 stations, every 3 h check)
  into `external_forecast`; `scripts/score_hii_forecast.py` scores HII, "no change" and our model on the *same* issue
  times at +24/48/72 h. First numbers after ~3 days; a fair verdict after ~2–4 weeks (≥ 15 issues).

## 3. Can our own model get better at 48 h? Two drivers tested

Honest split in both: fit only on data before the test window; test on the last 45 days (the production window).

### 3a. Upstream flow and dam release ([exp_48h_upstream](validation/exp_48h_upstream_2026-09-27.py))
48 h RMSE: CPY011 Ayutthaya 26.8 → **18.6 cm** (skill 0.27 → 0.49); CPY014 26.4 → 25.4; C.12 22.5 → 21.0;
CPY015 20.8 → 20.2; canal BKK021 no gain (46.2 vs 42.9, worse). Upstream matters where the river is not tide-dominated.

### 3b. Forecast rain ([exp_48h_rain](validation/exp_48h_rain_2026-09-27.py))
Rain history from Open-Meteo's archives (keyless): `historical-forecast-api` (≈ knowing the rain: an upper bound) and
`previous-runs-api` (`precipitation_previous_day1/2`: forecasts issued 1–2 days earlier — **the honest test**, slightly
worse than what we would really have). Honest 48 h RMSE (own model → own + rain):

| Gauge | 48 h all hours | 48 h, hours before ≥ 35 mm of rain |
|---|---|---|
| BKK021 Lat Phrao canal | 42.9 → **36.5 cm** | 66.4 → **50.9 cm** |
| C.12 Samsen (tidal river) | 22.3 → **18.1** | 43.5 → **22.7** |
| CPY014 Nonthaburi | 26.3 → **20.3** | 41.2 → **17.3** |

**Answer to "should the model use forecast rain?": yes.** It is the only input that improved a canal forecast, and the
gain is largest exactly when people need it (before heavy rain). Today rain is used only in wording and in the
"can't estimate recovery" rule, not in the level model.

## 4. Neighbourhood / space-time models ([exp_48h_neighbours](validation/exp_48h_neighbours_2026-09-27.py))

Same split; ridge regressions unless stated. "Network" = gauges upstream on the same river + C.13 dam release;
"proximity" = the 3 nearest long-record gauges by distance, any direction or water body.

| 48 h RMSE (cm) | No change | Own AR | **Network STAR** | Proximity STAR | k-NN analogue (k=25) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPY011 Ayutthaya | 36.6 | 36.0 | **18.6** | 25.6 | 25.8 |
| CPY014 Nonthaburi | 33.3 | 26.3 | **25.4** | 26.5 | 27.6 |
| C.12 Samsen | 35.9 | 22.3 | **21.0** | 22.7 | 24.4 |
| CPY015 Saphan Krung Thep | 41.7 | 20.7 | **20.2** | 20.4 | 21.2 |
| BKK021 Lat Phrao canal | 42.9 | 42.9 | 46.2 | 43.5 | 44.8 |

At 24 h the ranking is the same; proximity neighbours even *hurt* at C.12 (15.7 vs 14.1 cm own AR): they mix canals and
other basins in. **Follow the water, not the distance** — the owner's earlier point ("water varies by basin, not
distance", D-042) confirmed with forecast errors.

### Assessment of the families the owner listed

| Family | What it is | Fit for our data (≈ 100 gauges, 1 year hourly, 1 flood season, polders + pumps) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Space-time AR (STAR / ARX with network lags)** | Each gauge regressed on its own lags and on lagged neighbours | Tested: best at every river gauge; cheap; interpretable; per-gauge coefficients | ✅ **build next** (with rain, §3b) |
| **Spatial Stream-Network (SSN)** (Ver Hoef & Peterson) | Geostatistics with flow-connected ("tail-up") covariance | Made for *spatial prediction at unsampled points* on dendritic rivers, not for forecasting; needs a flow-connected network topology; Bangkok canals are looped, gated and pumped, so "downstream" is often undefined | ⚠️ later, only for along-river values between Chao Phraya gauges (D-019 allows along-river, never over land) |
| **Non-parametric k-NN analogue AR** | Find past states like now, average what followed | Tested: loses to linear STAR (one year has few flood analogues); may help with multi-year history and extremes | ⏸ revisit when history ≥ 3 years or with GloFAS reanalysis as analogue library |
| **GTWR** (geographically and temporally weighted regression) | Coefficients vary smoothly in space and time | Space: our per-gauge STAR already has site-specific coefficients, and space is not smooth across walls/polders (D-019). Time: the useful part is *recency weighting* — can be had by a rolling or weighted refit | ⏸ test "time-weighted refit" inside STAR instead of full GTWR |
| **ST-GNN** (spatio-temporal graph neural networks) | Learned message passing over a gauge graph | Data-hungry; one year and one flood season → high overfitting risk; hard to explain to citizens; Google Flood Hub-style LSTMs are trained on thousands of basins. The *graph idea* is kept (network STAR is its linear cousin) | ❌ not now; revisit with multi-year data, as a benchmark only |

## 5. Recommendation (next forecasting step, D-050)

1. **L3 "network STAR + rain"** per gauge: own tide/trend + upstream gauges and C.13 release (river) + archived rain
   forecasts (all gauges), trained on history, calibrated intervals (split-conformal on its own backtest, as today).
2. Keep it behind the existing gate: a gauge uses the new model only where it beats the current method **and** "no
   change" in the backtest, per horizon (GUIDELINES §2).
3. Start archiving what we need now: Open-Meteo `previous_runs` for each rain point (training data), HII FEWS forecasts
   (built), and C.13 discharge (already collected).
4. Show HII's forecast only after the scoring script shows it beats both "no change" and ours at that station and lead,
   labelled "คาดการณ์ของ สสน. (สมมติการระบายน้ำเขื่อนเท่าเดิม)".

## 6. Validation protocol (so "better" means something)

- **Rolling origin:** refit weekly, forecast forward, never train on the future; report per gauge × horizon.
- **Several windows:** at least 3 non-overlapping 30–45-day windows (wet and dry) before promoting a model.
- **Metrics:** RMSE/MAE and skill vs "no change"; interval coverage (90 % band must hold ~90 %); pinball loss; and
  **event scores** — hit rate / false alarms for "reaches the bank within 48 h", which is what people act on.
- **Heavy-rain subset** reported separately (§3b shows the gains concentrate there).
- **Same issue times** when comparing with HII (the scoring script does this).

## 7. Not done / limits

- One test window; no interval calibration for the experimental models yet; ridge only (no tuning).
- Rain features use point forecasts at the target gauge only; catchment rain upstream may do better.
- Pump and gate operations (BMA) are unknown to every model here — canal forecasts will stay the weakest.

## 8. Keep for the nationwide phase (owner, 2026-09-27: "the other methods might be useful outside Bangkok")

These results are Bangkok-specific: looped, gated, pumped canals and a tidal river. Outside Bangkok most rivers are
dendritic and unregulated or dam-controlled (APPROACH §19), which changes the ranking. Carry these forward to
[phase 5](../docs/plan/phase-5-nationwide.md):

| Method | Where it may fit nationally (flood types, APPROACH §19.2) | What to test first |
|---|---|---|
| Network STAR + rain | Everywhere a gauge has upstream gauges (F1 regulated, F2 unregulated rivers, F5 Mekong) | Same scripts on Nan/Yom/Ping chains and Chi/Mun (long HII/RID records exist) |
| **SSN** (stream-network geostatistics) | Dendritic rivers in the North and Northeast (F2, F4): values *between* gauges along a river, and virtual points for tier-B/C reaches | Needs a river-network topology (HydroSHEDS/MERIT Hydro); only along channels, never over land (D-019) |
| **k-NN analogue AR** | Slow, recurrent floods (F4 Chi/Mun backwater, F5 Mekong) with long records; GloFAS reanalysis (1984→) as an analogue library for tier-C | Compare with STAR on multi-year records; score "reaches warning level" events |
| **GTWR** | Large regions with smoothly varying behaviour (national rain-runoff response, flash-flood thresholds per tambon) | Spatially varying rain→rise coefficients vs DWR/HII flash-flood products (F3) |
| **ST-GNN** | A national graph of ~800 HII + 900 RID gauges with multi-year history is the scale these models need | Only as a benchmark against STAR, trained on multi-year national data; must pass the same skill gate and be explainable in the UI |

## 9. Built and proven: network STAR + rain in production (D-052, v0.8.0)
The owner said "continue" (Q32). The model from §5 is now a fourth candidate method, `star`, in the production
backtest (`forecast.evaluate`), per gauge and horizon:
- **Inputs:** own tide change, own 6 h / 24 h change, deviation from the 25 h mean; the 2 nearest gauges ≥ 5 km
  upstream on the Chao Phraya (by river km, skipping co-located gauges and the dam gauge); C.13 dam release for gauges
  downstream of the dam; forecast rain at the nearest rain point over the horizon (training: Open-Meteo previous runs,
  issued ~1–2 days before; live: the latest run). Canal gauges get rain only.
- **Rule:** ridge regression, trained only on targets ending before the 45-day test window; competes with the gauge's
  own methods **on the same rows**; used only where it wins and beats "no change" by the skill gate. Live: refit on
  all complete rows; if today's inputs are incomplete, fall back to the best own method with that method's range.
- **Validation** ([validate_star](validation/validate_star_2026-09-27.py), production code, 3 separate 45-day windows,
  every series cut at the window end):

| Window ends | Horizon | Gauges | `star` chosen | Median RMSE, best own → best incl. star | Gauges with skill ≥ 0.3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| now | 12 h | 77 | 48 | 15.2 → 12.6 cm | 36 → 43 |
| now | 24 h | 77 | 58 | 14.2 → 12.7 | 17 → 36 |
| now | 48 h | 77 | 63 | 26.7 → 22.1 | **8 → 35** |
| −45 d | 48 h | 76 | 43 | 18.1 → 16.8 | 10 → 20 |
| −90 d | 48 h | 76 | 37 | 22.6 → 20.9 | 8 → 20 |

- **Out of sample** (choose on the older window, score on the next, newer one — the honest test, since the table above
  picks and scores on the same window): where `star` was chosen, it stayed better than the gauge's own best method at
  **87–95 %** of gauges at 12 h, **90–93 %** at 24 h and **95–100 %** at 48 h (35/37 and 43/43), median gain 9.5–25 %.
- **Largest 48 h gains (latest window):** C.35 28.2 → 16.6 cm, CPY011 Ayutthaya 27.1 → 16.9, C.7A 54.4 → 34.7,
  CPY008 51.8 → 33.5, C.3 67.2 → 46.0 — the non-tidal upper river, as §3a predicted.
- **Bug found on the way:** gaps in a gauge's own record (CPY011: 158 readings in 2 days) made its 24 h change
  undefined at the latest hour, so the live path silently fell back. Own levels are now carried over gaps ≤ 6 h when
  building features (training and live alike; never in the target). Test: `test_star_still_forecasts_when_the_gauge_has_a_short_gap`.
- **Cost:** a full forecast run for 292 gauges takes ~145 s (every 30 min).
- **Limits:** one year of history; selection on a 45-day window; canal gains rely on rain alone (pumps and gates
  unknown); the interval "coverage" in the payload is still in-sample.
